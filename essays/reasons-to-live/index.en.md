Yesterday I was sorting through photos when I saw the news flooding my feed. A life extinguished. Severe depression.

I fell silent for a moment. Then I found a thought I'd written down long ago.

![Thought](images/004.jpg)

I realized: **being in a good mood is profoundly, deeply important.**

Before bed, I picked up a book called **"Reasons to Stay Alive."** The author is Matt Haig. It's wonderfully written.

I read it in one sitting and copied down some passages. Here they are — the first installment of my **"Good Mood Checklist."**

### Reasons to Stay Alive

**01**

You feel like you've landed on an alien planet, that no one can understand the pain you're enduring.

But in truth, they do. You think they don't because your only reference point is yourself. You've never experienced this kind of pain before — the shock of sliding into the abyss terrifies you. Yet others have been here too. **In that darkness, millions of people walk alongside you.**

**02**

You've already had suicidal thoughts. **Things can't get any worse. From here, the only way is up.**

**03**

**You hate yourself. That's because you're sensitive. I'd guess everyone could find a reason to hate themselves, if they thought about it as much as you do.** The truth is, **we are all bastards, and we are all beautiful angels.**

![Art](images/006.jpg)

**04**

You have a label — "depression." So what? Truth is, if you ask the right professional, **everyone gets a label.**

**05**

You feel like everything is going to get worse. But that feeling is just your symptom.

**06**

The mind has its own weather system. **Though you are in the middle of a tornado right now, the tornado's energy will eventually be spent. Hold on.**

![Art](images/007.jpg)

**07**

**Ignore the stigma.** Every illness has faced prejudice. We fear getting sick, and from that fear, stigma grows. Polio was once wrongly branded a disease of the poor. Depression is often dismissed as a "weakness" or a character flaw.

**08**

Nothing stays the same. This pain will not last forever. If the pain tells you it will, it's lying. Pain is a debt that can be repaid with time.

**09**

The mind changes. Personality changes. I wrote in *The Humans*: "Your mind is a galaxy. **There is more darkness than light, but the light is worth waiting for.** So don't end your life — even if the darkness is all there is. Know that life is not static. Time is also space. You move through the galaxy of time, **waiting for that star.**"

![Art](images/008.jpg)

**10**

One day, you will experience joy equal to this pain. You'll listen to the Beach Boys and cry tears of happiness. You'll lean down and gaze at a sleeping baby's face in your arms. You'll make many good friends. You'll taste food you've never tried before. You'll stand at a high place taking in a beautiful view, without thinking about the possibility of falling. There are still so many books you haven't read — they will enrich you. You'll eat oversized buckets of popcorn watching countless movies. You'll dance, laugh, make love, run along the riverbank, talk deep into the night, laugh until your stomach hurts. Life is waiting for you. Though you are briefly trapped here, the world isn't going anywhere.

If you can, hold on. Living is always worth it.

### Yue's Word

Reading this book sparked three lines of thought for me:

**01 A Shift in Perspective**

I once shared an insight in another article: **Lately I've been gradually replacing the thought "Why is this happening to me?" with "What is this trying to teach me?" — and I've found that everything around me has changed.** Reading books about treating depression, **the essence is really about shifting your perspective.**

![Art](images/010.jpg)

In "Reasons to Stay Alive," the author also writes a chapter about this:

"Those terribly bad days — though terrifying, though nearly impossible to survive — can be put to use later. Save them up. **Build a Bad Day Bank.** The day you had to flee the supermarket. The day you were so depressed you couldn't move your tongue. The day you made your parents cry. The day you almost jumped off a cliff. Then, when the next bad day comes, you can say: alright, today is bad enough, but there have been worse days before. **Even if today is the worst day you've ever had, at least you know the bank is still there. You've at least made a deposit.**"

![Art](images/011.jpg)

![Art](images/012.jpg)

**02 The People on the Other End of the Phone Are What Matter**

I once read that most people who choose suicide, despite their many different reasons, share one thing at the core: they feel the world has nothing to do with them anymore.

"The meaning of life lies in the people who love you. No one lives for an iPhone. It's the person on the other end of the phone who matters. Once we begin to recover, once we rediscover life's meaning, we grow a new pair of eyes. We see more clearly. We begin to notice things we could never perceive before."

![Art](images/013.jpg)

![Art](images/014.jpg)

![Art](images/015.jpg)

**03 Up & Down**

Life is full of ups and downs. The highlight moments are not the norm. He writes:

Yet, as time passed, I came to understand things I hadn't before. I learned that down is not the only direction. If you stay there, if you endure, things will get better. **They get better, then worse, then better again.**

As a homeopathic doctor told me when I was living at my parents' house: **"Peaks, valleys. Peaks, valleys." If you can, hold on. Living is always worth it.**

Let me also share a song I've had on repeat. **Xue Yue's "If There's Still Tomorrow."**

The story behind this song: the original singer was the "Godfather of Taiwanese Rock" in the 70s and 80s. In his thirties, he was diagnosed with terminal cancer. His friend Liu Weiren wrote this song for him. They had to record it three times — because the first two times, Xue Yue had to cry out all his tears before he had the strength to open his mouth and sing.

**"If there's still tomorrow, how will you dress your face? If there's no tomorrow, how do you say goodbye?"**

In those days before I made the decision to come to Beijing, I ran every day with this song looping in my headphones. Listen to the lyrics sometime.
